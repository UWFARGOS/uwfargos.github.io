The single most expensive mistake I see students make has nothing to do with
GPA, resume formatting, or interview skills. It is showing up on time to a
process that started a year earlier.

## The three clocks

Finance and accounting do not recruit on the same schedule, and neither of
them recruits on the schedule the university career calendar implies.

**Investment banking** runs earliest and fastest. Summer 2027 analyst
applications at most large banks opened in December 2025 and were effectively
closed by the end of January 2026. That is roughly eighteen months before the
internship starts, and the window is about eight weeks wide. If you are a
sophomore who plans to "start looking junior year," the seats are gone before
your junior year begins.

**Public accounting** runs on a fall cycle tied to the academic year, and it
is more forgiving. Firms post summer internships roughly a year ahead and
recruit rolling, which means early applicants see the most open seats but late
applicants still see some.

**Regional banks and corporate finance** run six months out or less. This is
the track where applying in the spring for a summer role still works.

## What this means depending on where you are

If you are a **freshman or sophomore**, your target is not a summer internship.
It is an early identification or "explore" program. These are pipeline programs
firms use to identify students before the main cycle, they are rarely
advertised, and they are the most realistic entry point if you are not at a
target school. Search the firm name plus "early insight" or "explore program."

If you are a **junior**, you are in the main cycle for accounting and late for
banking. Apply to accounting now and treat banking as a long shot that requires
networking rather than applications.

If you are a **senior**, look at full-time entry-level roles and federal
postings, which run continuously rather than in cycles.

## The practical move

Check the recruiting clock on the board every few weeks and note which windows
are open for your graduation year. Applying to a closed cycle is wasted effort,
and applying two weeks into an open one is worth more than a perfect resume
submitted in month three.

One habit worth building: when you find a firm you like, find their campus
recruiting page and note the month they posted last year. Cycles repeat. Last
year's date is a better predictor than anything else you have.
